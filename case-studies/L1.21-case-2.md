# Case study — L1.21 case 2: the recursive cascade

> **Source.** Cold-tier paper rev-4 → rev-5 → rev-6 → rev-7 → rev-8 in
> the seed session. Specifics redacted; the pattern is preserved.

> **Purpose.** Show L1.21 in its harder form: five consecutive
> revisions, each introducing a *new sub-class* of bug that escaped
> the prior version of the verification protocol. This case study is
> what motivated extending V1–V5 to V1–V6.

---

## Setup

A second research paper, separate from case 1. Standing reviewers same
as before (Claude variant + GPT variant, playbook 08). Reviewer B had
been holding the paper at MAJOR for 5 consecutive cycles over a
specific missing-experiment thread.

## Cycle v8 — rev-4: closes the 5-cycle thread; introduces 3 new A1 bugs

The revision agent successfully folded the missing experiment
(productive falsification — see `case-studies/productive-falsification-
case.md`). The headline finding strengthened materially. Reviewer B's
5-cycle MAJOR thread closed: reviewer B returned **MINOR** for the
first time.

But the same rev-4 introduced **three new arithmetic bugs**:

- A specific minimum-threshold value was reported as `N_min = 50K
  events` in 6 paper sections. The paper's own derivation gives 171K;
  the experiment's RESULTS § 6.3 says 147K. The 50K number was
  **fabricated** — neither value in the paper's own evidence base
  produced 50K.
- A corresponding "3× reduction" claim, derived from the wrong
  N_min, propagated through 5 sections.
- The fabrication cascaded into a closure-claim count
  ("all 7 prior failures closed") that became wrong under the
  corrected number.

Reviewer A (Claude variant) caught the fabrication. Verdict: reviewer A
**escalated from MINOR to MAJOR ESCALATED**. Reviewer B was at MINOR
(closing the 5-cycle thread).

**This is the verdict-swap event.** Same paper, same revision,
opposite verdict trajectories. Either reviewer alone would have
graduated rev-4: reviewer A alone would have stayed at MINOR forever
on the missing-experiment thread; reviewer B alone would have shipped
the fabricated N_min. See `case-studies/reviewer-disjointness-case.md`
for the disjointness pattern this case study exemplifies.

## Cycle v9 — rev-5: closes the rev-4 fabrication; one literal leftover

The next revision fixed the N_min fabrication everywhere the V1 grep
template caught: `50K`, `50,000`, `3× reduction`, `3-fold reduction`.
But one surviving phrase escaped: `minimum-N threshold drops 3× under
Regime A`. The agent had grepped for `50K` and `3× reduction` but
not for `drops 3×` or `3-fold`. Both reviewers caught the one-line
leftover; both verdict: MINOR with the same one-line fix.

**Lesson extracted into V1's template list:** add `drops 3×`,
`3-fold`, and any near-synonym variants to the grep template. V1
templates accumulate across the project's lifetime.

## Cycle v10 — rev-6: closes the literal leftover; cross-section inconsistency

The next revision fixed the line-N leftover. But the agent computed a
worked-example table using **Regime-A constants** while the paper
*everywhere else* defined the operating regime as **Regime B with
inherited constants**. The worked-example table was internally
consistent (V1–V2 passed narrowly), but **cross-section inconsistent**
with the paper's stated formula.

The result: reviewer A verified the table's internal arithmetic and
returned ACCEPT. Reviewer B noticed the cross-section drift and
returned MINOR — catching that under the paper's stated formula the
residual would be much larger in the opposite direction, not the
small positive value the table reported.

**This is the case study that motivated V3.** V1–V2 are per-section;
V3 is cross-section. The lesson was named `L1.22` (cross-section
inconsistency, a sibling failure mode to L1.21). After this cycle, the
revision-agent prompt required V3 explicitly: for every load-bearing
constant introduced or modified, enumerate all other sites that depend
on it and re-derive each.

## Cycle v11 — rev-7: closes the cross-section; denominator convention drift

The next revision reconciled the formula correctly. The reconciliation
was honest and substantive: the prior revision's "all 7 failures
closed" claim was downgraded to "5 of 8 closed; 3 near-threshold
pending" under the corrected formula.

Reviewer A: ACCEPT with 3 cosmetic nits.

Reviewer B: MINOR, but caught a new bug class. Residuals were computed
as `residual / predicted` whereas the historical convention (used
in the paper's earlier sections and in the source experiment) was
`residual / measured`. Under `/measured`, the residuals shift by 3–4
percentage points and the multi-cell pass-count drops by one. **Same
load-bearing direction; different denominator.**

**This is the case study that motivated extending V4.** V4 had
required single-denominator recomputation; the extension required
computing residuals under *both* conventions and confirming the
convention matches the paper's stated convention. The lesson was
named `L1.23`.

The denominator failure is particularly insidious because the
*direction* of the residual is preserved (−25% under one convention,
−36% under the other); only the magnitude shifts, which can pass a
directional sanity check.

## Cycle v12 — rev-8: graduation under V1–V6

All five v11 issues closed under V1–V6 (V1–V5 plus the extended V4
denominator audit). Both reviewers returned ACCEPT.

This was the **first UNANIMOUS ACCEPT in the entire 23-day session**.
The L1.21 cascade had run for five revisions; V1–V6 closed it on the
sixth.

## Pattern summary

The L1.21 bug class shrinks by roughly an order of magnitude in
surface area at each recurrence:

1. **rev-4:** N_min fabrication propagating across 6 sections.
2. **rev-5:** one literal leftover phrase.
3. **rev-6:** one closure-table worked example using wrong constants.
4. **rev-7:** one normalisation choice changing magnitudes (not signs).
5. **rev-8:** none (UNANIMOUS ACCEPT).

L1.21 is not a single bug class — it is a **pattern of regression** in
agent-authored revisions. As one scale of bug is closed, the
next-smallest scale becomes the marginal failure mode. The discipline
that breaks L1.21 must therefore be a **layered audit** that closes
each scale of bug before it surfaces in the next review cycle.

## What V3 and V4-extended caught that V1–V2 didn't

| V1–V2 catches | V3 catches | V4-extended catches |
|---|---|---|
| `50K` survives | The worked example uses constants from regime A, but the formula 30 lines away assumes regime B constants | Residual −25% under `/predicted`, residual −36% under `/measured` — same direction, different magnitude |
| `3× reduction` survives | A constant `175.3` is used in one section, but a formula elsewhere requires `332` to be used here | The closure-count drops from 8/12 to 7/12 under the alternate denominator |

V1–V2's strength is **per-section**. V3's strength is
**cross-section**. V4-extended's strength is **convention-aware
recomputation**. The three together close the bug classes that
escaped earlier protocol versions.

## Lessons extracted

1. **L1.21 is not bounded above by V1–V2.** Two new bug classes
   (cross-section inconsistency, denominator drift) escaped V1–V2
   and required dedicated protocol steps.
2. **The protocol is monotone-extensible.** V3 was added after L1.22;
   V4 was extended after L1.23. V7 is anticipated as "narrative
   consistency" (abstract claim vs body claim drift).
3. **Reviewer disjointness was load-bearing.** The verdict-swap at
   v8 (reviewer A: MAJOR-ESCALATED, reviewer B: MINOR-closes-thread)
   is the single cleanest demonstration that single-reviewer protocols
   are insufficient. See `case-studies/reviewer-disjointness-case.md`.
4. **The productive-falsification cycle (the new experiment in rev-4)
   was simultaneously the cause of the L1.21 cascade** — the new
   experiment introduced new content that introduced new bugs. This
   is normal: substantive expansion always creates some debt. The
   discipline is to expect it and structure V1–V6 to catch it.

## What to do if you see this pattern

The recursive cascade is what L1.21 looks like in its mature form.
The mitigation is the same as in case 1, plus:

- **Watch for L1.22 (cross-section drift)** specifically when a
  revision introduces a worked example, a closure table, or any
  artifact that references *constants* defined elsewhere. Apply V3
  before commit.
- **Watch for L1.23 (denominator drift)** specifically when a
  revision touches *residuals, normalisations, or comparisons*.
  Apply V4-extended (both conventions) before commit.
- **Don't expect 1 protocol cycle to close the cascade.** Each
  protocol extension closes one new bug class. Plan for 2–3 cycles
  of monotone protocol extension before reaching UNANIMOUS ACCEPT.
